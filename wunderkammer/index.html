In the collection

A rhinoceros no one had seen.

In 1515 a rhinoceros arrived in Lisbon, the first in Europe for twelve hundred years. Dürer never saw it. He drew it from a letter and a sketch, and this woodcut became the rhinoceros for the next three centuries.

Scroll to cut the block
01 · The line

Cut by hand, line by line.

Every line here was left standing by a woodcutter's knife; everything else was cut away. Watch them return in the order your eye finds them.

Pear wood block, about 21 × 30 cm. Printed in thousands of copies.
02 · The ink

Armour that wasn't there.

Dürer gave it plates like a suit of armour, a scaly hide and a small extra horn on its back. None of them are real, and almost everyone believed them anyway.

Even school textbooks used Dürer's rhinoceros until the 1930s.
03 · The legacy

The most copied animal in art.

It is in the museum in the original printing, alongside the letters that described the animal to him.
