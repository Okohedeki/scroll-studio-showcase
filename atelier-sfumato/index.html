A lesson in five parts

Paint the Mona Lisa with us, one layer at a time.

Scroll, and watch the most famous portrait in the world come together the way it was actually made: proportion, line, value, underpainting, then colour.

Scroll to begin
Lesson 01 / 05

Proportion

Before a single line, find the big shapes. A vertical axis, the head as an oval, the whole figure inside a triangle.

Leonardo set the two horizons at different heights. It makes her seem to rise out of the landscape.
Lesson 02 / 05

Line

Now draw what you see, not what you know. Start where the viewer will look first, the face, then the hands, then everything else.

Keep the charcoal loose. Lines are questions until the value answers them.
Lesson 03 / 05

Value

Squint until the detail disappears and only light and dark remain. Build the shadows in layers, following the form like a hand following a cheek.

Sfumato means 'gone up in smoke': edges that melt, never lines that stop.
Lesson 04 / 05

Underpainting

Masters laid in the whole picture in one earth colour first. It settles every value before colour can confuse them.

Raw umber dries fast and stays warm under every glaze.
Lesson 05 / 05

Colour

Thin, transparent glazes, dozens of them, each one barely there. Colour doesn't cover the drawing, it tints it.

Leonardo worked on this panel for well over a decade. You can take your time.
